2024

The Challenges and Prospects of Cotton Production in Gombe State: A Political Economy Perspective

Saleh, A. & Muhammad, A. · Jewel Journal of Politics, Vol. 2, No. 2, pp. 135–147
Problem
Cotton was once one of Gombe State’s main sources of income and rural employment, but production has been in decline.
Approach
A descriptive survey grounded in Marxist political economy theory, using a Chi-square test on 351 returned questionnaires from a sample of 360 cotton farmers drawn from 5,721 registered farmers.
Finding
Insecurity from armed attacks, poor seedlings and pesticides, and unfavourable pricing are the leading causes of the decline. The paper recommends coordinated action from government, private agencies, and individuals to revive the sector.

Climate Change in Africa: Emerging Impacts and Policy Challenges

Muhammad, A. · Article · Applied Worldwide
Problem
Climate change threatens African development across agriculture, water resources, and public health.
Approach
A policy-focused article reviewing the continent’s exposure to climate impacts and its institutional response.
Finding
Limited institutional capacity and financing are the key barriers to adaptation, and sets out the policy challenges facing African governments.

2021

Nigerian Foreign Policy and the Issue of Image Building: A Critical Assessment of Muhammadu Buhari’s Administration (2015–2019)

Saleh, A. & Muhammad, A. · BRICS Journal of Educational Research, Vol. 11, Issue 1, pp. 5–9
Problem
Nigeria’s international image has suffered from terrorism, corruption, and leadership challenges.
Approach
Secondary-source thematic and content analysis, using James Rosenau’s Linkage Theory (1969), assessing the Buhari administration.
Finding
Buhari’s foreign policy centred on “shuttle diplomacy”, seeking international support against Boko Haram and for the economy. It partly restored Nigeria’s image, but corruption, unemployment, human rights concerns, and insecurity kept weighing it down.

The Role of Technology and Innovation in Tackling Insecurity and Accelerating Development in Nigeria

Muhammad, A. · BRICS Journal of Educational Research, Vol. 11, Issue 2, pp. 71–75
Problem
Rising insecurity (Boko Haram, banditry, kidnapping, pipeline vandalism, farmer-herder clashes) alongside stalled economic development.
Approach
Secondary-source review using Neo-Malthusian theory.
Finding
Technology and innovation can help Nigeria reduce insecurity and diversify its economy, but brain drain and skills shortages hold this back. Recommends investing in youth skills and equitable resource allocation.

2021

Medical Brain Drain: Causes and Implications in Nigeria’s Health Care System

Muhammad, A. & Aji, S. · BRICS Journal of Educational Research, Vol. 11, Issue 3, pp. 105–109
Problem
The migration of doctors and nurses from Nigeria to wealthier countries is weakening an already strained health system.
Approach
Secondary-source thematic and content analysis, using dependency (underdevelopment) theory.
Finding
Identifies “push” factors at home and “pull” factors abroad. Brain drain worsens service delivery and economic backwardness; recommends addressing the structural, political, and economic causes to work toward a “brain gain”.

2020

Nigeria and the Dilemma of Global Image: A Comparative Analysis of Goodluck Jonathan and Muhammadu Buhari Administrations’ Foreign Policy (2011–2019)

Saleh, A. & Muhammad, A. · IOSR Journal of Humanities and Social Science, Vol. 25, Issue 11, pp. 5–13
Problem
Nigeria’s international image has long been undermined by governance and security failures.
Approach
Comparative content analysis of the Jonathan (2011–2015) and Buhari (2015–2019) administrations’ foreign policy, using Traits Theory.
Finding
Buhari’s anti-corruption stance and fight against Boko Haram had more international impact than Jonathan’s “shuttle diplomacy”, though persistent corruption, police brutality, and insecurity kept weighing on Nigeria’s image. Recommends stronger institutions and resource management.

2024

Catalysts of Change: Tackling the Climate Challenge Together

Muhammad, A. · Book chapter · Catalyst of Change: Navigating Climate Change Together, Nasmedia Publisher, Indonesia, pp. 10–14
Problem
Climate change is accelerating: melting ice caps, rising temperatures, extreme weather, and biodiversity loss (IPCC, 2021), and communities need workable models to respond.
Approach
A review of best practices grouped into three areas: decarbonization, adaptation strategies, and building a resilient future, drawing on IPCC, REN21, IRENA, World Bank, and UN sources.
Finding
Cutting emissions, adapting infrastructure and ecosystems (flood defences, reforestation), and committing to frameworks like the Paris Agreement give communities a practical path through the climate crisis. Closes with Gandhi’s line: “The future depends on what we do in the present.”
